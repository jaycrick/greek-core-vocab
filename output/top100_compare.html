How to read this

Both top-100 lists are ranked by their own frequency measure, then percent is computed against that list's own top-100 sum as denominator — not the full corpus. master_vocab.csv ranks by combined_weighted_frequency (Perseus weighted frequency, scaled where a Plato volume overlaps the corpus, plus corpus lemma counts). The corpus ranking sums raw lemma-token counts across all 49 one/<work_id>/lemma.tsv files (492,766 tokens, 12,232 distinct lemmas total), the same skip-punctuation/skip-unmatched rule corpus_vocab.py uses per work.
  • 78 of the top 100 lemmas appear on both lists.
  • The single biggest driver of the other 44: genre. greek-core-vocab spans 121 works across epic, tragedy, comedy, history, oratory and philosophy (Homer through Marcus Aurelius); vocabulary-corpus-prep's own ranking here is aggregated across all 49 of its works, but every one of them is Attic prose — history, oratory, philosophy, no verse at all (its own CORPUS.md). A word common in verse but rare in prose ranks well in one list and not the other.
  • 22 appear only in greek-core-vocab's top 100 — several (τῷ, τῇ, εἶμι, οὔτε, δεῖ, εἶπον) occur only a handful of times across all 49 corpus-repo works — consistent with genre, not a lemmatization mismatch.
  • 22 appear only in vocabulary-corpus-prep's top 100 — common prose words (θεός, δίκαιος, ἀκούω) and proper nouns concentrated in Thucydides (Ἀθηναῖος, Σωκράτης from Plato) that place well within a 49-work prose corpus but don't crack the master list's top 100 once diluted across 121 works of every genre.
